Entering retirement mortgage-free used to be close to a default expectation; today, carrying some mortgage debt into retirement is increasingly common, whether from a later first home purchase, a second mortgage taken on for a renovation or family support, or simply a longer loan term. Whichever situation applies to you, it is worth turning this into a deliberate decision rather than an assumption.

## Why this matters more heading into retirement

Once regular employment income stops, mortgage repayments have to be funded from NZ Super, KiwiSaver drawdown, and other savings — sources that are generally more limited and less flexible than a salary. A mortgage payment that was comfortably affordable during your working years can represent a much larger proportion of a reduced retirement income, which is why clearing or substantially reducing mortgage debt before retirement is generally a priority worth actively planning for, not just hoping works out.

## Strategies for accelerating repayment in your final working years

**Lump-sum repayments.** If you have other savings outside KiwiSaver — term deposits, shares, an inheritance — putting a portion toward the mortgage in the years immediately before retirement can make sense, particularly if the mortgage interest rate is higher than what those other savings are earning after tax. This is a genuine trade-off worth calculating with real numbers rather than assuming one option is automatically better.

**Increasing regular repayments.** If your income has grown since you first took out the mortgage but your repayments have stayed the same, increasing them — even modestly — in the final 5 to 10 years before retirement compounds meaningfully given how much of a smaller remaining balance is closer to being paid down.

**Refinancing to a shorter term.** As covered in our related article on mortgage strategy in mid-life, shortening the remaining loan term at a refix is often more achievable than people assume, particularly once you are further into the loan and your income is typically higher relative to the remaining balance.

**Using KiwiSaver strategically.** While KiwiSaver access starts at 65, using it specifically to clear a small remaining mortgage balance at that point — rather than treating it purely as ongoing income — is a legitimate strategy some people use, though it should be weighed against the loss of that capital's ongoing growth and its role in later drawdown.

## The trade-off against other retirement savings

Aggressively paying down a mortgage in your final working years is not automatically the right priority over building other retirement savings — it depends on your mortgage interest rate compared to realistic investment returns, your risk tolerance, and how much flexibility you want in retirement. Someone with a low, well-fixed mortgage rate might reasonably prioritise KiwiSaver or other investment contributions instead, while someone with a higher rate or a strong preference for being debt-free may prioritise the mortgage. This is worth modelling explicitly rather than defaulting to either option out of habit.

## If full repayment is not realistic

Not everyone will clear their mortgage before retirement, and that is not automatically a crisis — but it does need to be planned for explicitly. If you are heading into retirement with a remaining balance, incorporate the ongoing repayment directly into your retirement income drawdown plan (see our related article) as a fixed cost, rather than assuming it will somehow resolve itself. In some cases, downsizing to a smaller, mortgage-free property becomes part of the plan specifically to address a remaining balance that cannot realistically be cleared through repayments alone.

## The general principle

Whichever path applies to your situation, the key shift in your 50s and early 60s is moving from "the mortgage will get paid off eventually" to an explicit plan with a realistic date and clear strategy attached — because once regular income stops, the flexibility to adjust reduces considerably, and it is far easier to solve this while you are still earning than after you have stopped.
