Most young workers do not know their basic employment rights — which means they sometimes get less than they are entitled to, or put up with things they do not have to. This guide covers what the law actually says.

## Your employment agreement

Every employee in NZ must have a written employment agreement before they start work. This is a legal requirement. If an employer has not provided one, they are breaking the law.

Your agreement must include:
- The names of you and your employer
- A description of your work
- An indication of where you will work
- The agreed hours (or an indication of the arrangements)
- The wage or salary
- What will happen if there is a dispute

Read it before you sign. You are entitled to take it away to consider, and you can seek independent advice. Citizens Advice Bureau (cab.org.nz) offers free employment agreement advice.

## Minimum wage

As of April 2026, the NZ minimum wage is $23.15 per hour for adults (18 and over). There is a starting-out wage ($18.52) and training wage ($18.52) that apply in limited circumstances — mainly for employees under 20 in their first 6 months or in industry training.

Your employment agreement cannot pay you less than minimum wage, regardless of what it says.

Check Employment New Zealand (employment.govt.nz) for current rates — they are reviewed annually on 1 April.

## Rest and meal breaks

You are entitled to paid rest breaks and unpaid meal breaks depending on how long your shift is:

- Under 2 hours: no break required
- 2 to 4 hours: one 10-minute paid rest break
- 4 to 6 hours: one 10-minute paid rest break
- 6 to 8 hours: one 10-minute paid rest break and one 30-minute unpaid meal break
- Over 8 hours: two 10-minute paid rest breaks and one 30-minute unpaid meal break

These breaks must be at reasonable times — not at the very start or end of a shift. Your employer decides the timing unless you agree otherwise.

## Leave entitlements

**Annual leave** — you are entitled to 4 weeks of paid annual leave per year. Annual leave accrues from day one but can generally only be taken after 12 months of employment (though employers can agree to let you take it earlier).

**Sick leave** — from the start of your employment, you are entitled to 10 days of paid sick leave per year. You can use sick leave from day one. Unused sick leave can be carried over (up to 20 days total).

**Public holidays** — if you work on a public holiday, you are entitled to time-and-a-half pay plus an alternative holiday (a day off in lieu). If a public holiday falls on a day you would normally work and you do not work, you are entitled to a paid day off.

**Bereavement leave** — 3 days paid leave for the death of an immediate family member (spouse, parent, child, sibling, grandparent). 1 day for other people if you had a close relationship with them.

**Parental leave** — primary carers are entitled to up to 26 weeks of paid parental leave (government-paid) after 12 months of employment.

## Trial periods

Employers with 19 or fewer employees can offer a 90-day trial period to new employees. During this period, you can be dismissed without the usual grounds and without the right to raise a personal grievance about the dismissal.

Trial periods must be agreed in writing before you start work. They cannot be added after you have started. They do not affect any of your other rights — you still have minimum wage, leave entitlements, and the right to a safe working environment.

Employers with 20 or more employees cannot use 90-day trial periods.

## A safe workplace

Your employer is legally required to provide a safe working environment under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015. This includes:
- Identifying and managing hazards
- Providing appropriate training and equipment
- Not requiring you to do work that puts you at unreasonable risk

If you are asked to do something that feels unsafe, you have the right to refuse. You should not be penalised for raising safety concerns.

## If something feels wrong

If you believe your employer is breaking the law or treating you unfairly, you have options:

**Talk to your employer first** — many issues can be resolved directly. Put the conversation in writing (follow up an in-person conversation with an email summarising what was discussed).

**Contact Employment New Zealand** — free advice at employment.govt.nz or 0800 209 020.

**Join a union** — unions provide free advice and can represent you in disputes. Many industries have relevant unions.

**Personal grievance** — if you believe you have been unjustifiably dismissed or treated unfairly, you can raise a personal grievance. You generally must do this within 90 days of the event. Mediation is free through Employment New Zealand. Unresolved disputes go to the Employment Relations Authority.

You cannot be fired for raising legitimate concerns about your employment conditions or safety. Retaliation for raising concerns is itself grounds for a personal grievance.
