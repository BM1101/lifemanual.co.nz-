Tax is one of those topics that sounds complicated but is actually straightforward for most people in their first few years of working. Here is what you actually need to know.

## Do you need to file a tax return?

In New Zealand, most people who earn only from salaried or waged employment (PAYE — Pay As You Earn) do not need to file a tax return. Your employer deducts tax before paying you, and Inland Revenue (IR) automatically calculates at the end of the year whether you paid too much or too little and sends you the difference.

**You do need to file an IR3 (individual income tax return) if you:**

- Have income from self-employment, freelancing, or contracting
- Have rental income
- Have income from overseas
- Receive income not taxed at source (investments, dividends, etc.)
- Earn over $200 from interest and dividends combined
- Are claiming certain expenses or deductions

If you are only earning from one employer in standard employment, check your myIR account — IR may have already done everything for you.

## The myIR account

myIR (ird.govt.nz) is your online Inland Revenue account. This is where everything happens. Create an account if you do not have one — you will need your IRD number and identity verification.

In myIR you can:
- See your tax summary for any year
- Check whether IR has calculated a refund or bill
- File an IR3 if required
- Update your bank account details for refunds
- Manage KiwiSaver

## The tax year

The NZ tax year runs from 1 April to 31 March. The return for the year ending 31 March 2026 is due by 7 July 2026 (unless you have a tax agent, in which case the deadline is extended).

## If IR calculates your return automatically

Log into myIR after April each year. If you only have PAYE income, IR will have prepared an automatic income tax assessment. Review it — check the income amounts look right, check your bank account details are correct for any refund. If everything looks correct, you confirm it and any refund is paid into your account within a few days.

## If you need to file an IR3

Filing online through myIR is straightforward. The form walks you through each income source.

**What you will need:**

- Your total income from each source (your employer provides a summary — look for an Employment Information Summary or check myIR)
- Any expenses you are claiming (if self-employed)
- Details of any rental income and expenses
- Any overseas income

**Common things people miss:**

- Bank interest — banks report interest to IR, but it is worth checking it is all captured
- Freelance or cash income — this is taxable even if no one is withholding tax on it
- Working for Families tax credits — if you have children, you may be entitled to these

## Refunds and bills

If you overpaid tax during the year, you will receive a refund. If you underpaid, you will receive a bill.

Common reasons for overpayment (refunds):
- You had more than one job and the secondary employer deducted at the wrong rate
- Your income was irregular and tax was deducted at a rate higher than your actual rate
- You worked only part of the year

Common reasons for underpayment (bills):
- You had untaxed income (freelance, rental, interest) that you did not account for during the year
- You did not update your tax code when your circumstances changed

## Tax codes

When you start a job, you complete a tax code declaration (IR330). Your tax code tells your employer what rate to deduct tax at. The standard code for a main job is M (or M SL if you have a student loan). Secondary jobs use S or SH.

Getting this wrong is one of the most common reasons for an unexpected tax bill or refund. Check your IR330 is correct when you start any new job, especially if you have multiple sources of income.

## Student loans

If you have a student loan, IR automatically deducts repayments at 12 cents in the dollar on income above the repayment threshold ($24,128 as of 2026). This is handled through PAYE automatically once your employer knows you have a student loan — make sure your tax code includes SL.

## Getting help

**IR helpline:** 0800 775 247
**myIR online help:** extensive guides at ird.govt.nz
**Tax agents:** if your situation is complicated (self-employment, rental property, overseas income), a tax agent or accountant is worth the fee — they can often find deductions that more than pay for themselves.

Filing your own return through myIR is genuinely straightforward for most people. The key is not to put it off — late returns attract penalties.
