Peer pressure and conflict are part of being human, but they feel especially intense in your early teens. This guide gives you practical tools — actual phrases you can use.

## Understanding peer pressure

Most peer pressure is subtle. Everyone else seems to be doing something, so you feel you should too. A friend asks you to do something and you feel bad saying no. Someone makes a comment implying you are being boring.

When you feel pressured, ask yourself: if no one was watching, would I want to do this? If the answer is no, the only reason you are considering it is to manage what other people think of you.

## Scripts for saying no

**The simple no:** "Nah, I'm good." Delivered confidently, many people just accept it.

**The blame-shift:** "My mum/dad would kill me." A socially acceptable exit that does not invite debate.

**The redirect:** "I'm not into that — want to do something else instead?"

**The honest version:** "I'm not really comfortable with that." Harder to say but builds more respect.

## Handling conflict

**Wait until you have calmed down** before confronting someone. Things said in anger are harder to take back.

**Use "I" statements** — "I felt left out when you did not invite me" lands differently than "You always leave me out."

**Say what you need** — "I felt hurt — I just need to know we are still good" is more productive than just expressing what upset you.

**When someone is upset with you** — listen before you defend yourself. Acknowledge their feelings before explaining your perspective.

## Drama

The most effective way to stay out of drama: do not say anything about someone that you would not say to their face. "I don't really want to get involved" is a complete sentence.

Drama tends to surround people who engage with it. People who decline to participate become known as trustworthy — which is a much better social position.
