Knowing basic first aid is one of the most useful things you can learn. In an emergency, the first few minutes matter enormously — and the person closest is usually not a doctor.

## The most important thing: call 111

In New Zealand, 111 is the emergency number for police, fire, and ambulance. In any serious emergency, call it immediately. The dispatcher will talk you through what to do. Stay on the line.

## CPR

CPR is used when someone has stopped breathing and their heart has stopped.

1. Check the scene is safe, then check if the person is responsive — tap their shoulder and shout "Are you okay?"
2. If no response, call 111
3. Lay the person on their back on a firm, flat surface
4. Place the heel of one hand on the centre of their chest, place your other hand on top, and interlock your fingers
5. Press down firmly — about 5 to 6 centimetres — and release. Do 30 compressions at about 100 to 120 per minute
6. After 30 compressions, tilt the head back, lift the chin, pinch the nose, and give 2 rescue breaths
7. Continue until the ambulance arrives

If you are not comfortable with rescue breaths, compression-only CPR is still significantly better than nothing.

## Serious bleeding

1. Apply firm, continuous pressure directly to the wound using whatever you have
2. Do not remove the material if it soaks through — add more on top
3. Keep applying pressure for at least 10 minutes
4. Raise the injured limb above the level of the heart if possible
5. Call 111 if bleeding is severe or does not slow

## Burns

1. Remove from danger, remove clothing near the burn if not stuck
2. Cool with cool (not cold) running water for 20 minutes — this is the most important step
3. Do not use ice, butter, or toothpaste — they make burns worse
4. Cover loosely with cling wrap
5. Call 111 or go to ED for any burn larger than a 50-cent coin

## Choking

If the person can cough, let them — a cough is more effective than anything you can do.

If they cannot cough, speak, or breathe:
1. Call 111
2. Give 5 back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand
3. Give 5 abdominal thrusts — stand behind them, fist just above the navel, pull sharply inward and upward
4. Alternate until dislodged or they lose consciousness
5. If they lose consciousness, begin CPR

Consider doing a St John First Aid course — they offer Youth First Aid programmes throughout New Zealand.
