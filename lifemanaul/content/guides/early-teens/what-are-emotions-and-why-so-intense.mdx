If you have ever felt something so intensely that it seemed out of proportion, there is a reason for it. And it is not that you are oversensitive or dramatic.

## What is happening in your brain

Your brain is under construction. The teenage brain is going through the most significant period of development since infancy.

The brain develops from back to front. The limbic system — the emotional centre — develops relatively early. The prefrontal cortex — responsible for reasoning, impulse control, and regulating emotions — is the last part to fully develop. It is not fully mature until the mid-twenties.

What this means in practice: you have a fully developed emotional accelerator and a still-developing braking system.

## The role of the reward system

The teenage brain has a particularly responsive reward system. Experiences that feel good produce a stronger dopamine response in teenagers than in adults. This is also why peer opinions feel so important — the part of your brain that processes social evaluation is more active during adolescence than at any other time in life.

## Why small things feel huge

When your limbic system fires a strong emotional response and your prefrontal cortex is still developing the ability to moderate it, small things genuinely can feel huge. Research using brain scanning shows that teenagers show significantly more limbic activity in response to emotional stimuli than adults. Teenagers are not imagining that things feel more intense — they are.

## Working with intense emotions

**Name what you are feeling** — research shows that labelling an emotion activates the prefrontal cortex and reduces the intensity of the limbic response. Simply saying "I feel embarrassed" takes some of the power away.

**Create space before you respond** — give yourself a few minutes before responding to a message, walk away from a confrontation, sleep on a decision. This allows the prefrontal cortex to catch up.

**Talk to someone** — articulating an experience to another person is one of the most effective ways to process it.

## This does not last forever

The teenage years are genuinely one of the most emotionally intense periods of life. But it is also temporary. As your prefrontal cortex continues to develop through your late teens and twenties, emotional regulation becomes significantly easier. It is not a character flaw. It is a brain that is still being built.
