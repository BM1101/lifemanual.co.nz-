If you struggle to fall asleep, find yourself exhausted in the morning, or feel like you could sleep until noon on weekends, you are not lazy. There is a biological reason for all of it — and there are practical things that actually help.

## How much sleep do teenagers need?

Teenagers need between 8 and 10 hours per night. This is not a preference. During puberty, your brain is developing rapidly and sleep is when most of that development happens.

## Why teenagers naturally stay up late

During puberty, a biological shift happens in your circadian rhythm. The release of melatonin shifts to about two hours later than in childhood, meaning your body genuinely does not feel tired until later at night. This is called sleep phase delay and it is real, documented biology — not laziness.

## What gets in the way of sleep

**Screens** — phones emit blue light that suppresses melatonin and signals to your brain that it is daytime. This is the biggest sleep disruptor for most teenagers.

**Caffeine** — coffee and energy drinks have a half-life of about 5 to 6 hours. A coffee at 4pm still has half its caffeine in your system at 10pm.

**Irregular sleep times** — sleeping in on weekends confuses your body clock.

**Stress and anxiety** — if your mind is racing when you lie down, sleep becomes much harder.

## What actually helps

**Keep consistent sleep and wake times** — the most evidence-backed sleep intervention. Going to bed and waking at the same time every day regulates your body clock more effectively than anything else.

**Put your phone in another room** — hard but effective. The bed should be associated with sleep.

**Wind down for 30 minutes before bed** — dim the lights, read a book, do something calm.

**Keep your room cool and dark** — your body temperature drops as you fall asleep. Around 18 degrees helps.

**Get outside in the morning** — morning light is the most powerful signal your body clock has.

**Avoid caffeine after 2pm** — this gives it time to clear your system before bed.
