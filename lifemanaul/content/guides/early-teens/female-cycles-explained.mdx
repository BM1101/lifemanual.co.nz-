If you have recently got your period, or are expecting it soon, this guide explains everything in plain English — how it works, what to expect, and when to ask for help.

## What is a period?

Every month, your uterus prepares for a possible pregnancy by building up a lining of blood and tissue. If no pregnancy occurs, that lining is shed through the vagina. This is your period. It is a sign that your reproductive system is working as it should.

## How long is a cycle?

A menstrual cycle is measured from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. The average cycle is 28 days, but anywhere between 21 and 35 days is normal. When periods first start, they are often irregular — sometimes skipping months entirely. This is normal and usually settles down within one to two years.

## How long does a period last?

Most periods last between three and seven days. The flow is usually heavier in the first couple of days and lighter toward the end.

## What does the blood look like?

Period blood can range from bright red to dark brown or even almost black, especially at the start or end. This is all normal. Small clots up to about the size of a 50-cent coin are also normal on heavier days.

## Managing period pain

Mild to moderate cramps are common and can usually be managed with:
- Over-the-counter pain relief such as ibuprofen
- A heat pack on the lower abdomen
- Gentle movement or walking
- Rest

If pain is severe enough to stop you going to school or doing normal activities, talk to a doctor.

## Period products

**Pads** — worn in underwear, absorbing blood outside the body. A good starting point.

**Tampons** — inserted into the vagina. Change every 4 to 8 hours, never longer than 8 hours.

**Period underwear** — absorbent underwear that replaces or supplements other products.

**Menstrual cups** — small silicone cups inserted into the vagina. Can be worn for up to 12 hours.

## When to see a doctor

Talk to a doctor if:
- Your period has not started by age 16
- Periods are extremely heavy
- You have severe pain that does not respond to pain relief
- Your cycle is consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days
- Your period suddenly stops for more than three months

In New Zealand, you can call Healthline on 0800 611 116 any time for free health advice.
