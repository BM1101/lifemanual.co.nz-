The internet is one of the most useful tools in human history. It is also a place where decisions made in a few seconds can have consequences that last for years.

## What is a digital footprint?

Your digital footprint is the record of everything you do online — posts, photos, comments, accounts, searches. The key principle: assume that anything you post online could eventually be seen by anyone.

## Why it matters more than you think

Employers and universities routinely search candidates online. A photo or comment that seemed funny at the time can create a bad first impression years later. Screenshots of private messages get shared. Posts made within a friend group have a way of getting out.

## Practical habits that protect you

**Use strong, unique passwords** — long, random, not used anywhere else. Use a password manager like Bitwarden (free).

**Turn on two-factor authentication** — even if someone gets your password, they cannot get into your account without a second verification. Turn it on for email, Instagram, Snapchat, gaming accounts.

**Check your privacy settings** — most platforms default to more public than most people realise.

**Think before you post** — would you be comfortable if your grandparent, future employer, and worst enemy all saw this?

**Do not share personal information publicly** — your school, suburb, or daily routine does not need to be public.

## Cyberbullying and online harassment

If someone is saying something hurtful or threatening about you online:
- Take screenshots as evidence
- Block and report the person
- Do not respond — engaging usually makes it worse
- Tell a trusted adult

In New Zealand, contact Netsafe at netsafe.org.nz or 0508 638 723 for free, confidential advice.

## If someone asks for photos or personal information

Any adult who asks a teenager for photos of themselves is behaving inappropriately, regardless of how they seem. Do not respond, block them, and tell a trusted adult immediately.
