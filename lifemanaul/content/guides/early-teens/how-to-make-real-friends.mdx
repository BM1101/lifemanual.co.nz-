Social media has made it easy to accumulate connections and hard to build real ones. This guide is about building the kind of friendships that actually make a difference in your life.

## What makes a friendship real?

**Reciprocity** — both people make an effort. If you are always the one reaching out, that is important information.

**Honesty** — you can say what you actually think without fear of the friendship ending.

**Showing up** — particularly when things are hard. A friend who only wants to spend time with you when things are fun is not the same as one who checks in when you are struggling.

**Consistency** — real friendship is built over time through repeated interactions.

## How to actually make friends

**Be somewhere consistently** — most friendships form through repeated proximity. Joining a club, sport, or activity puts you in the same place as the same people regularly.

**Be genuinely curious about others** — ask people questions and actually listen to the answers. Being a genuinely attentive listener is rarer than you think, and people remember it.

**Be the one who reaches out** — most people are waiting for someone else to initiate. Being the person who does it is not desperate — it is confident.

**Show up when it matters** — if a friend is going through something hard, reach out. You do not have to say the perfect thing.

**Be yourself** — presenting a curated version of yourself attracts people who like that version. Being genuine attracts people who like you.

## Quality over quantity

Research on wellbeing consistently shows that the quality of your close relationships matters more than the number of them. Having three or four people you can be honest with and count on is more valuable than fifty casual acquaintances.

If you feel lonely, the answer is not more followers. It is investing more deeply in the one or two people already in your life.
