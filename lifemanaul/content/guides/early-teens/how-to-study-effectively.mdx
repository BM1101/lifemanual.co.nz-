Most students study by re-reading their notes and hoping it sticks. Research on learning consistently shows this is one of the least effective methods available.

## Why re-reading does not work

Re-reading creates a feeling of familiarity — the material looks familiar, so your brain tells you that you know it. But familiarity is not the same as being able to recall and use information under pressure. Re-reading is passive. Your brain is not working hard to retrieve the information.

## What actually works: active recall

Active recall means testing yourself — trying to retrieve information from memory rather than recognising it when you see it.

**How to do it:**
- Cover your notes and try to write down everything you remember about a topic
- Use flashcards — question on one side, answer on the other
- Do past exam papers without looking at your notes first
- Explain the topic out loud as if teaching someone who knows nothing about it

The moment of struggling to retrieve information is when the learning is happening.

## Spaced repetition

Review material just before you are about to forget it. A simple version: review material the day after first learning it, then three days later, then a week later. Each time you successfully recall it, the interval gets longer. Apps like Anki automate this process.

## Other effective techniques

**The Feynman Technique** — explain the concept in the simplest possible language, as if to a child. When you get stuck, go back to your notes and learn what you are missing.

**Eliminate distractions** — just having your phone visible (even face-down) reduces cognitive capacity. Put it in a different room.

**Study in chunks** — the Pomodoro Technique: 25 minutes of focused work, 5-minute break, repeat.

## Before an exam

Do not cram the night before. Sleep is when your brain consolidates memories — going into an exam well-rested is more valuable than an extra hour of studying at midnight.

Do past papers under exam conditions — timed, no notes, in one sitting. This is the most accurate simulation of the actual exam.
