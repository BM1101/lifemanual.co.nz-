Feeling anxious or overwhelmed sometimes is part of being human. But if it is happening a lot, or stopping you from doing things you want to do, there are practical strategies that genuinely help.

## What is anxiety?

Anxiety is your body's alarm system. When your brain perceives a threat, it triggers a physical response: heart rate speeds up, muscles tense, breathing quickens. The problem is that the brain does not always distinguish between real physical danger and social or emotional threat.

Anxiety becomes a problem when it is disproportionate to the situation, happens frequently without a clear cause, or stops you from doing things you want or need to do.

## In-the-moment techniques

**Controlled breathing** — breathe in for 4 counts, hold for 4, breathe out for 6. The longer exhale activates the parasympathetic nervous system, which counters the fight-or-flight response. Do this for 2 to 3 minutes.

**The 5-4-3-2-1 grounding technique** — notice 5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell, and 1 you can taste. This pulls your attention into the present moment.

**Cold water** — splashing cold water on your face slows the heart rate. It works.

**Moving your body** — even a short walk can significantly reduce acute anxiety.

## Longer-term strategies

**Face the things that make you anxious, gradually** — avoidance feels like relief short-term, but teaches your brain the thing is genuinely dangerous. Gradual exposure is the most effective way to reduce anxiety over time.

**Challenge anxious thoughts** — ask: Is this definitely true? What is the evidence? What would I say to a friend thinking this?

**Protect your sleep** — sleep deprivation and anxiety are closely linked.

**Limit social media** — heavy social media use is consistently linked with increased anxiety in teenagers.

## When to ask for help

Talk to someone if anxiety is happening most days, stopping you from going to school or seeing friends, or if you are having thoughts of hurting yourself.
