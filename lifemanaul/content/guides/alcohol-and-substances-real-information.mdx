This guide gives you real, factual information about alcohol and common substances — not scare tactics, not moralising. The goal is that you can make genuinely informed decisions.

## Why this matters more for teenagers

Before anything else, one piece of context that genuinely changes the picture: the teenage brain is still developing, and it is more vulnerable to the effects of alcohol and other substances than an adult brain.

The prefrontal cortex — the part responsible for decision-making, impulse control, and judgment — is not fully developed until the mid-twenties. Regular substance use during adolescence can affect this development in ways that have lasting consequences. This is not an exaggeration — it is well-established in neuroscience research.

This does not mean every teenage experimentation causes permanent damage. But it does mean the risks are meaningfully higher for teenagers than for adults, and that information is worth having.

## Alcohol

**What it does:** Alcohol is a depressant — it slows the central nervous system. In small amounts it reduces inhibition and creates feelings of relaxation. In larger amounts it impairs coordination, judgment, and memory. In very large amounts it can cause loss of consciousness, respiratory depression, and death.

**The unit system:** One standard drink in NZ contains 10 grams of pure alcohol. A can of beer (330ml, 5%) is 1.3 standard drinks. A glass of wine (150ml, 13%) is 1.5 standard drinks. A shot of spirits (30ml, 40%) is one standard drink.

**Blood alcohol and the law:** In NZ, the legal blood alcohol limit for driving is 0.05% for adults and 0.00% (zero) for anyone under 20. Alcohol significantly affects driving ability well before the legal limit — reaction time, judgment, and night vision are all impaired.

**The real risks:** Most alcohol-related harm for teenagers comes from binge drinking (consuming large amounts in a short time) rather than moderate use. Binge drinking increases the risk of accidents, assault (both being a victim and a perpetrator), unsafe sexual decisions, and alcohol poisoning.

**Alcohol poisoning:** Signs include confusion, vomiting, unconscious or unconscious-ish, blue or pale skin, slow or irregular breathing. This is a medical emergency. Put the person in the recovery position (on their side), call 111, and stay with them.

**NZ law:** The legal purchase age for alcohol is 18. It is illegal for someone under 18 to buy alcohol, or for anyone to supply alcohol to someone under 18 in a public place without their parent's consent.

## Cannabis

**What it does:** Cannabis contains THC, which binds to receptors in the brain and alters perception, mood, memory, and coordination. Effects include relaxation, altered sensory perception, increased appetite, and in some cases anxiety or paranoia — particularly in people who are prone to anxiety or with stronger strains.

**The risks:** Cannabis affects the developing brain differently from the adult brain. Regular use in adolescence is associated with effects on memory, attention, and IQ, particularly for heavy users. It can trigger or worsen anxiety and psychosis in people with a predisposition. It impairs driving.

**NZ law:** Cannabis is illegal to possess, use, or supply in New Zealand. A referendum on legalisation was held in 2020 and voted against.

## Vaping and nicotine

**What it does:** Vaping delivers nicotine (and other chemicals) through aerosol. Nicotine is highly addictive and affects brain development in teenagers.

**The risks:** Nicotine addiction is real and hard to break. The teenage brain becomes dependent on nicotine more quickly than the adult brain. While vaping is less harmful than cigarettes for long-term users switching from smoking, it is not harmless — particularly for young people who would not otherwise use nicotine.

**NZ law:** It is illegal to sell vaping products to anyone under 18.

## Stimulants and party drugs

Substances like MDMA (ecstasy), cocaine, and amphetamines affect the dopamine and serotonin systems in the brain. They carry risks including dehydration (particularly relevant for MDMA at events), cardiovascular effects, and psychological dependence. Unknown substances sold as one thing frequently contain something else.

## Harm reduction

The most important harm-reduction information:

- Do not use substances alone — have people you trust around you
- Do not mix substances, including with alcohol — interactions are unpredictable
- Do not drive, swim, or do anything requiring coordination or judgment while impaired
- If something goes wrong, call 111 — police will generally not prosecute someone who calls for help in a medical emergency
- Test kits are available to check substances for common adulterants — they are legal to purchase in NZ

## Getting help

If you are concerned about your own or someone else's use of alcohol or other substances:

- **Alcohol Drug Helpline**: 0800 787 797, text 8681, or chat at alcoholdrughelp.org.nz — free, 24/7, confidential
- **1737**: text or call for free counselling
- **Your GP**: can discuss concerns confidentially

You will not get in trouble for asking for help.
