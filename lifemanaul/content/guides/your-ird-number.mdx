An IRD number (Inland Revenue Department number) is your unique tax identifier in New Zealand. Every person who earns money in NZ needs one — it is how the government tracks tax.

You will need your IRD number before you start your first job. Employers are required to deduct PAYE (Pay As You Earn) tax from your wages, and they need your IRD number to do that correctly. Without one, they must deduct tax at the highest possible rate — which means you would be paying more tax than you should.

## What is it used for?

Your IRD number is used for:

- Employment — every employer needs it to process your pay correctly
- KiwiSaver — your contributions are tracked against your IRD number
- Bank accounts — some accounts require it, particularly interest-bearing savings accounts
- Filing tax returns
- Any government benefit or payment

Think of it like a student ID number, but for your entire financial life. You only ever have one IRD number, and it stays with you for life.

## How to apply

The easiest way is online at ird.govt.nz. The process takes about 10 minutes.

**What you will need:**

- Your full legal name
- Your date of birth
- Your current NZ address
- A valid NZ identity document — a NZ passport, NZ birth certificate, or NZ citizenship certificate
- Your bank account number (optional but recommended — used for any refunds)

**Steps:**

1. Go to ird.govt.nz
2. Click on "Register" or search for "Apply for an IRD number"
3. Select "New Zealand resident or citizen"
4. Fill in your personal details
5. Upload or photograph your identity document
6. Submit

You will receive your IRD number by post within about 8 to 10 working days. If you need it urgently (for a job starting soon), call Inland Revenue on 0800 227 774 and explain the situation — they can often process it faster.

## Can I apply before I have a job?

Yes, and you should. You can apply for an IRD number at any age. Applying before you need it means you will have it ready when your first job comes through — rather than scrambling at the last minute.

## Keeping your IRD number safe

Your IRD number is personal information that identifies you in the tax system. You should:

- Keep a note of it somewhere secure
- Give it to employers when asked — this is normal and required
- Not share it unnecessarily with people or organisations who do not need it

You do not need to memorise it, but having it written down somewhere safe (like a notes app with a strong password, or in a filing system at home) means you can find it when you need it.

## What about tax?

Once you have an IRD number and start working, tax is generally handled automatically through PAYE — your employer deducts the right amount of tax before paying you. You do not usually need to file a tax return unless you have multiple sources of income or significant untaxed income.

Inland Revenue also automatically calculates whether you have paid too much or too little tax at the end of the year and sends you a refund or bill accordingly — for most people this happens without them having to do anything.

If you do need to file a tax return (called an IR3), see our guide on that when the time comes.
