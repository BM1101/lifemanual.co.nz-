Many New Zealanders live together without getting married, assuming their legal situation is simply "not married." In reality, after a qualifying period, de facto relationships carry very similar legal rights and obligations to marriage.

## What is a de facto relationship?

Under the Property (Relationships) Act 1976, a de facto relationship is one where two people are not married but are living together as a couple in a genuine domestic relationship.

## The 3-year threshold

The Act applies to de facto couples who have been together for at least 3 years. This threshold can be reached earlier if you have a child together, or if one person has made a significant contribution to the relationship.

## What is relationship property?

When a qualifying de facto relationship ends, relationship property is divided equally — 50/50 by default.

Relationship property broadly includes: the family home regardless of whose name it is in, property acquired by either partner during the relationship, KiwiSaver contributions made during the relationship, and income earned during the relationship.

Separate property broadly includes: property owned before the relationship, and property received as a gift or inheritance if kept separate.

## The family home

Even if only one partner's name is on the title, the family home is generally treated as relationship property and divided 50/50.

## Contracting out agreements

A contracting out agreement lets both parties agree to different rules for how property would be divided. For it to be enforceable: both parties must have received independent legal advice, and both must have signed in front of a lawyer.

## Getting advice

This is an area where the cost of professional advice is almost always worth it relative to what is at stake. A family lawyer or Citizens Advice Bureau can help.
