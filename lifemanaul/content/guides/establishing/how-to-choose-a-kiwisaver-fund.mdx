Most New Zealanders are in the wrong KiwiSaver fund — either a default conservative fund they never actively chose, or an expensive actively managed fund.

## The fund types

**Conservative** — mostly bonds and cash. Low risk, low growth. Appropriate within 2 to 5 years of needing the money.

**Balanced** — mix of growth and defensive assets. A middle ground that is rarely optimal for any specific situation.

**Growth** — mostly shares (70 to 90%). Higher short-term volatility, significantly higher long-term return.

**Aggressive** — almost all shares. Highest expected long-term return.

## What most people in their 20s and 30s should be in

If you are more than 10 years from either buying your first home or turning 65: a growth or aggressive fund. Over 20 to 40 years, shares significantly outperform bonds and cash. The mistake is switching to conservative when markets fall — that locks in losses and misses the recovery.

## The impact of fees

$50,000 balance, $3,000 per year contributions, 7% gross return, over 30 years:
- At 0.5% annual fee: approximately $508,000
- At 1.5% annual fee: approximately $427,000

The difference is $81,000 — on the same underlying investments. Fees are the most controllable variable in long-term KiwiSaver performance.

## How to compare providers

Use sorted.org.nz/tools/kiwisaver-fund-finder. Compare annual fund charges, historical returns over 5 and 10 years, and provider stability.

Well-regarded lower-fee options include Simplicity (not-for-profit, index-based, among the lowest fees in NZ), SuperLife, and Kernel.

## When to shift to conservative

For a first home purchase: move to conservative 2 to 3 years before your expected purchase date. For retirement: gradually shift in the 5 to 10 years before turning 65.
