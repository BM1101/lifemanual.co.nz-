Most people never ask for a pay rise. Of those who do, most ask for less than they should. Both are mistakes that compound over a career.

## Step 1: Know your market rate

Before any conversation, know what your role is worth. Sources: Seek.co.nz salary insights, Trade Me Jobs salary guide, LinkedIn salary insights, industry associations. Come with a specific number backed by market data.

## Step 2: Build your case

Document specific achievements since your last review, new responsibilities taken on, skills developed, positive feedback received. Quantify where possible — numbers are more compelling than adjectives.

## Step 3: Timing

Good timing: during or just after a performance review, after completing a significant project, when you have a competing offer, when the business is doing well.

Poor timing: during organisational stress, just after a mistake, when your manager is under unusual pressure.

## Step 4: The conversation

Request a meeting in advance. Open positively. Make your case with specific examples. Then state your number: "Based on my contributions and market data, I would like to move my salary to $X." Then stop talking. Resist the urge to fill the silence.

## Step 5: Handling the response

If they say yes — get confirmation in writing including the effective date.

If they say "we will think about it" — set a follow-up date.

If they say no — ask: "What would I need to achieve to get there?" Get specific criteria and a timeline.

## The compounding effect

A $5,000 pay rise at 28 is worth dramatically more than just $5,000 — every subsequent raise and future employer offer is anchored to your current salary. The discomfort of one 15-minute conversation is small relative to what is at stake.
