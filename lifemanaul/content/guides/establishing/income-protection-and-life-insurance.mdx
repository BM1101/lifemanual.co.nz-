## The principle of insurance

Insurance exists to cover financial risks you cannot self-fund. Before buying any insurance, ask: if this bad thing happened, could I manage without it? If yes, you may not need it. If no, insurance is justified.

## Income protection insurance

IP insurance pays a percentage (usually 75%) of your income if you cannot work due to illness or injury. In your 30s, you are far more likely to be unable to work than to die — which is why IP is often more valuable than life insurance.

Key features to understand:

**Agreed value vs indemnity** — agreed value pays the agreed benefit regardless of income at claim time. Indemnity pays based on actual income at claim time (which may be lower). Agreed value is better but more expensive.

**Wait period** — the period between becoming unable to work and when payments start. Longer wait period means lower premiums. If you have sick leave and savings to cover 13 weeks, choose a longer wait period.

**Own occupation vs any occupation** — own occupation pays if you cannot do your specific job. Any occupation only pays if you cannot do any job. Own occupation is far more valuable.

ACC covers accident-related injuries but not illness. IP fills this gap.

## Life insurance

Life insurance pays a lump sum when you die. You genuinely need it when you have children depending on your income, or a partner who could not manage the mortgage alone.

A rough guide: enough to pay off the mortgage and provide several years of income replacement.

## Things you probably do not need

Mortgage protection insurance (expensive for the cover provided), credit card or loan repayment insurance (poor value, exclusion-heavy), extended warranties on appliances.

A fee-for-advice financial adviser (who does not earn commission) can provide more neutral guidance than one paid by commission. MoneyHub.co.nz and sorted.org.nz provide independent NZ-specific guidance.
