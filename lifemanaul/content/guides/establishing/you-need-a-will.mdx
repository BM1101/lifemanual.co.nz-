Most people know they should have a will. Very few people under 40 actually have one. The consequences of not having one can be significant — particularly if you have a partner, children, or any assets at all.

## What happens if you die without a will

Dying without a will (intestate) means the Administration Act 1969 determines who inherits your assets through a fixed hierarchy — spouse or partner first, then children, then parents, then siblings. This may not match your wishes. If your de facto relationship is not legally established, your partner may get nothing.

## What a will does

A will lets you choose your beneficiaries, appoint an executor, appoint guardians for your children, leave specific gifts, and state your funeral wishes.

## How to get a will

**Through a lawyer** — the most reliable method. A straightforward will costs $150 to $400. Particularly important if your situation is complex.

**Online will services** — Public Trust and Perpetual Guardian offer online preparation. Good for straightforward situations.

## Making your will valid

Must be in writing, signed by you, and witnessed by two people present at the same time. Witnesses must not be beneficiaries.

## Keeping your will up to date

Review when you marry (marriage automatically revokes a previous will in NZ), separate or divorce, have children, or buy significant assets.

## Where to keep your will

Keep the original with your lawyer, in a bank's safe custody, or at home in a fireproof document box. Tell your executor where it is.
