A mortgage is the largest financial commitment most people ever make. Understanding how it actually works changes how you think about every decision in the process.

## What a mortgage is

A loan where the property itself is the security. If you stop making repayments, the bank can sell the property to recover what they are owed. Most NZ buyers need at least a 20% deposit.

## How repayments work

In the early years of a mortgage, almost all of your repayment goes to interest — not to reducing the actual debt. On a $600,000 loan at 6.5% over 30 years, your monthly repayment is roughly $3,800. In the first month, approximately $3,250 goes to interest and only $550 reduces your loan balance.

This is why extra repayments early are so powerful — every extra dollar reduces the balance that interest is calculated on.

## Key terms

**Fixed rate** — locked for a set period (1 to 5 years). Provides certainty but less flexibility.

**Floating rate** — moves with the market. Allows extra repayments without break fees.

**Offset account** — a savings account linked to your mortgage. The balance reduces the interest you pay.

## What the bank looks at

Income and serviceability, existing debts, your deposit, credit history, employment stability, and the property itself.

## The total cost

$600,000 at 6.5% over 30 years: total repaid approximately $1,365,000, total interest approximately $765,000. Understanding this number — and how extra repayments reduce it — is one of the most useful things you can do before committing.

## Reducing your total interest

Pay fortnightly instead of monthly — this effectively makes one extra payment per year. Make extra repayments when possible. Use a mortgage broker — they compare rates across multiple lenders and are paid by the bank, not you.
