## Sole trader vs company

**Sole trader** — no separate registration required. Simplest and cheapest. Business income is your personal income. You are personally liable for any debts.

**Limited liability company** — a separate legal entity. Provides liability protection. Company income taxed at 28%. More ongoing compliance. Worth considering when turnover is substantial or liability risk is a concern.

For most side businesses starting out, sole trader is the right starting point.

## Setting up as a sole trader

No formal registration required. Notify IRD that you are self-employed through myIR. Apply for a GST number if required. Keep records of income and expenses. File an IR3 at year end.

## GST registration

You must register for GST if your turnover reaches $60,000 in any 12-month period. Once registered, add 15% GST to invoices, collect it from customers, claim back GST on business expenses, and file regular GST returns.

## Provisional tax — the surprise

This catches most first-year self-employed people. Employees have tax deducted automatically. Self-employed people pay their own tax in advance. In your first year you pay nothing — then at year end you receive a potentially large tax bill, and in year two you start paying in instalments.

Practical advice: set aside 28 to 33% of every invoice into a separate account. Do not touch it. When the tax bill arrives, the money is there.

## Business expenses you can claim

Equipment and tools, software subscriptions, professional development, business travel, home office costs (proportion of rent, power, internet), professional fees, marketing costs. The expense must be incurred for the purposes of the business. Keep all receipts.

## Getting an accountant

For most new self-employed people, an accountant for the first year is worth the cost. They often save more in tax than their fee costs.
