## What an index fund is

A market index is a list of companies grouped by some characteristic — like the NZX 50 or the S&P 500. An index fund automatically holds all the companies in a given index, in the same proportions. No human fund manager decides what to buy or sell — the fund tracks the index mechanically.

## Why index funds outperform most active funds

Active funds employ professional managers who research companies and decide what to buy. The problem: after fees, more than 90% of actively managed funds underperform their comparable index over 15-year periods.

The fee gap compounds this. A 1.5% active fund versus a 0.25% passive fund over 30 years on $100,000 produces a gap of approximately $90,000 in final balance — even if both funds achieve identical returns before fees.

## Where to access index funds in NZ

**Sharesies** — Smartshares ETFs and international ETFs. Beginner-friendly.

**InvestNow** — no platform fee. Vanguard funds and other managed index funds.

**Kernel** — NZ-built, focused on index funds. Around 0.25% annual fee.

**Hatch** — direct access to US-listed ETFs including Vanguard and iShares products.

## What to actually buy

For most NZ investors: a global index fund. Options include the Kernel Global 100, Vanguard Total World ETF (VT) on Hatch, or Smartshares Total World ETF on Sharesies.

## One fund is enough

A single global index fund already holds thousands of companies. Adding more funds usually adds complexity without adding meaningful diversification. Start simple.
