You do not need to become a good cook. You need to be able to feed yourself without spending all your money on takeaways. These 10 recipes require almost no prior experience, basic equipment (a pot, a pan, a knife, a chopping board), and ingredients available at any supermarket.

## Before you start: the basics

**A sharp knife** makes cooking dramatically easier and safer than a blunt one. If cutting vegetables feels like a struggle, the knife probably needs sharpening.

**Season as you go** — adding a little salt at each stage of cooking develops more flavour than adding it all at the end.

**Taste as you cook** — the only way to know if something needs more salt, acid (lemon, vinegar), or spice is to taste it.

**Mise en place** — a French phrase meaning "everything in its place." Prepare all your ingredients before you start cooking. Chop everything, measure things out, have everything ready. Cooking is much less stressful this way.

## 1. Scrambled eggs

**Why:** Eggs are cheap, quick, high in protein, and eat-able at any time of day.

**Ingredients:** 2 to 3 eggs, splash of milk, butter, salt and pepper.

**Method:** Crack eggs into a bowl, add a small splash of milk and a pinch of salt. Whisk until fully combined. Heat a pan on medium-low, add a small knob of butter. Pour in eggs. As they start to set, push them gently with a spatula in slow folds. Remove from heat while still slightly underdone — they continue cooking from residual heat. Serve immediately.

**Key tip:** Low heat and patience. High heat makes rubbery eggs.

## 2. Pasta with a basic tomato sauce

**Why:** Fast, cheap, filling, and infinitely adaptable.

**Ingredients:** Pasta (any shape), 1 can crushed tomatoes, 2 cloves garlic, olive oil, salt, dried herbs (oregano or basil).

**Method:** Boil pasta in heavily salted water until just cooked (al dente). While pasta cooks, heat olive oil in a pan, add minced garlic and cook 1 minute until fragrant (do not burn). Add canned tomatoes, herbs, salt. Simmer 10 minutes. Drain pasta, combine with sauce. Parmesan on top if you have it.

**Key tip:** Salty pasta water is not optional — it seasons the pasta from the inside.

## 3. Stir-fry

**Why:** Ready in 15 minutes, uses whatever vegetables you have, works with any protein.

**Ingredients:** Any protein (chicken breast, beef strips, tofu, eggs), any vegetables, soy sauce, garlic, ginger, oil, rice or noodles.

**Method:** Cook rice or noodles separately. Heat a pan or wok until very hot. Add oil, then protein — do not move it for a minute so it browns. Add garlic and ginger. Add vegetables (hardest ones first — carrot, broccoli — then softer ones like capsicum and zucchini). Add soy sauce. Toss, serve over rice or noodles.

**Key tip:** Hot pan is everything. A lukewarm pan makes things stew rather than fry.

## 4. Fried rice

**Why:** Uses leftover rice and whatever vegetables or protein you have — almost free to make.

**Ingredients:** Cooked rice (day-old works best), 2 eggs, any vegetables, soy sauce, garlic, oil.

**Method:** Heat oil in a pan until hot. Add garlic, cook briefly. Add vegetables, cook until tender. Push everything to the side, crack eggs in and scramble. Add rice, break up any clumps. Add soy sauce and toss everything together.

**Key tip:** Fresh rice is too wet — leftover rice from the fridge makes better fried rice.

## 5. Roast chicken thighs

**Why:** Incredibly forgiving to cook, cheap, delicious, and the oven does all the work.

**Ingredients:** Chicken thighs (bone-in, skin-on are cheapest and most flavourful), olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder, any other spices you like.

**Method:** Preheat oven to 200°C. Pat chicken dry with paper towel (important — moisture prevents crisping). Rub with olive oil, season generously with salt, pepper, garlic powder. Place skin-side up on a roasting tray. Roast 35 to 40 minutes until skin is golden and juices run clear.

**Key tip:** Pat dry, high heat, skin-side up. That is all you need for crispy skin.

## 6. Vegetable soup

**Why:** Uses up any vegetables about to go off, feeds multiple people cheaply, keeps well in the fridge.

**Ingredients:** Any vegetables (onion, carrot, potato, celery, zucchini — whatever you have), stock (chicken or vegetable), garlic, olive oil, salt.

**Method:** Dice vegetables. Cook onion and garlic in olive oil until soft. Add harder vegetables (carrot, potato), then softer ones (zucchini). Pour over stock to cover. Simmer until everything is tender, 20 to 30 minutes. Season, add herbs if you have them.

**Key tip:** Start with onion and garlic as a base — this builds flavour for everything you add after.

## 7. Toasted sandwich or quesadilla

**Why:** Three-minute meal with minimal dishes.

**For a toasted sandwich:** Two slices of bread, butter, cheese, any filling. Butter the outside of the bread. Fill, press together, toast in a pan over medium heat, pressing down with a spatula. Flip when golden.

**For a quesadilla:** Tortilla, cheese, any filling (leftover chicken, beans, salsa). Heat a dry pan, add tortilla, add filling on one half, fold over. Cook until golden on both sides.

**Key tip:** Do not rush — medium heat gives even browning without burning.

---

## 8. Baked potato

**Why:** Almost zero effort, filling, and endlessly adaptable with toppings.

**Method:** Preheat oven to 200°C. Wash potato, dry it, prick with a fork several times. Rub with oil and salt. Place directly on oven rack, bake 45 to 60 minutes until the skin is crispy and a fork slides easily into the centre.

**Toppings:** Butter and cheese, baked beans, sour cream and chives, whatever you have.

**Key tip:** Prick the potato before baking — skipping this risks it exploding in your oven.

## 9. Banana pancakes

**Why:** Two ingredients, ready in 5 minutes, no dairy or flour required.

**Ingredients:** 1 ripe banana, 2 eggs.

**Method:** Mash banana thoroughly. Whisk in eggs until smooth. Heat a pan on medium with a little oil or butter. Pour small circles into the pan. Cook until bubbles appear and edges look set, flip, cook another minute.

**Key tip:** Keep them small — they are fragile. A few tablespoons per pancake is enough.

## 10. Lentil dal

**Why:** Extremely cheap, high protein, keeps well, and takes about 25 minutes.

**Ingredients:** 1 cup red lentils, 1 can coconut milk or 400ml stock, 1 can crushed tomatoes, onion, garlic, ginger, curry powder or cumin and turmeric, oil, salt.

**Method:** Cook onion in oil until soft. Add garlic, ginger, and spices, cook 1 minute. Add lentils, tomatoes, and liquid. Bring to a boil, reduce heat, simmer 20 minutes until lentils are completely soft and the mixture has thickened. Season well. Serve over rice.

**Key tip:** Red lentils need no soaking and cook quickly. Rinse them before adding.
