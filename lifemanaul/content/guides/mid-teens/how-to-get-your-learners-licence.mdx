Getting your licence is a major milestone — and the first step is the learner licence. Here is everything you need to know about getting it in New Zealand.

## When can you apply?

You can apply for your learner licence from age 16. There is no minimum time you have to wait after turning 16 — you can apply on your birthday if you want.

## What is a learner licence?

A learner licence (sometimes called an L licence) is the first stage of the NZ graduated driver licensing system (GDLS). It lets you practise driving, but with strict conditions:

- You must always have a supervisor in the front passenger seat
- The supervisor must hold a full NZ licence (not restricted) and have held it for at least 2 years
- You must not drive between 10pm and 5am
- You must not use a mobile phone while driving (including hands-free)
- You must display L plates
- Your blood alcohol limit is zero

You must hold your learner licence for at least 6 months before you can sit the restricted licence test.

## Step 1: Study the road code

The learner licence test is based on the NZ Road Code. You need to know road rules, signs, and safe driving practices.

**Free resources:**
- The official NZ Transport Agency (NZTA) Road Code is available free at nzta.govt.nz
- The DriveTest app (free, available on iOS and Android) has practice tests that mirror the actual questions

**Paid resources:**
- Road Code books are available at bookshops and online for around $25 to $30
- The AA offers an online learner licence preparation course

Take practice tests until you are consistently getting 34 to 35 out of 35. The actual test is easier when you have overlearned the material.

## Step 2: Sit the test

The learner licence test is taken at an NZTA-approved testing agent — most AA branches, many Vehicle Testing NZ (VTNZ) centres, and some other locations offer it.

**What the test involves:**

- 35 multiple-choice questions
- You need at least 32 correct to pass (91%)
- The test is on a computer at the testing centre
- It takes about 30 to 45 minutes
- Cost: $43.90 (as of 2026, check nzta.govt.nz for current fees)

**What to bring:**
- Acceptable identity documents (passport, birth certificate, or NZ citizenship certificate)
- Proof of your address if your ID does not include it
- The test fee (credit or debit card accepted at most locations)

**On the day:** Read each question carefully. Some questions are deliberately worded to catch people who skim. If you are unsure, eliminate the obviously wrong answers first.

If you fail, you can resit — but you will need to pay again. Most people pass on their first attempt if they have done sufficient practice tests.

## Step 3: Get your licence card

Once you pass, you will be issued a learner licence card. This also serves as a valid form of photo ID in New Zealand.

## Practising on your learner

The 6 months you spend on your learner licence is your opportunity to build real driving skill. Research consistently shows that new drivers who practise more hours are significantly safer.

The NZTA recommends at least 120 hours of practice before sitting your restricted licence test, including at least 10 hours of night driving. This is not a legal requirement — but it reflects what the evidence shows about developing genuine competence.

Use these 6 months well:
- Practise in a range of conditions — rain, night, motorways, rural roads
- Get comfortable with parking, reversing, and multi-lane driving
- Do not just practise the routes you know — unfamiliar roads build better skills

## After the learner: the restricted licence

After 6 months (or 3 months if you have completed an approved driving course), you can sit your restricted licence test. This is a practical on-road test with an examiner.

The restricted licence comes with its own conditions — most notably that you cannot carry passengers between 10pm and 5am unless they are supervised by a full licence holder. See our guide on the restricted licence when you are ready for that step.

## A note on costs

Getting your licence costs money beyond the test fee:

- Road Code book: $0 to $30
- Learner licence test: $43.90
- Learner licence card: included in test fee
- Driving lessons (optional but useful): $60 to $100 per hour typically

Professional driving lessons are not required but can be valuable, particularly for building confidence with specific skills like motorway driving or parallel parking.
