Your CV is not a biography. It is a marketing document with one job: to get you an interview. Everything on it should serve that purpose.

## How long a CV should be

For entry-level roles (under 2 years experience): one page. For more experienced candidates: two pages maximum. Three pages is almost never appropriate.

Hiring managers at busy companies spend an average of 7 seconds scanning a CV before deciding whether to read it properly. Your CV needs to communicate clearly and quickly — not comprehensively.

## The format that works

Use a clean, simple layout. No photos, no graphics, no coloured text. Black text on white background. A readable font (Calibri, Arial, or Georgia) at 10 to 12 point.

**Standard sections, in order:**

**Contact details** — full name, phone number, email address, LinkedIn URL (if you have one), location (city is enough — not your full address).

**Professional summary (optional)** — two to three sentences summarising who you are and what you bring. Only include this if you can write something genuinely useful rather than generic. "Motivated self-starter with excellent communication skills" tells the reader nothing.

**Work experience** — list in reverse chronological order (most recent first). For each role: job title, company name, dates (month and year), and three to five bullet points describing what you did and achieved.

**Education** — school, qualification, year completed. Include relevant grades or achievements if strong.

**Skills** — a brief list of relevant technical skills (software, languages, tools). Do not list soft skills like "team player" — demonstrate them through your experience instead.

**Other (optional)** — volunteer work, extracurriculars, or interests if they are genuinely relevant or impressive.

## Writing your work experience

The weakest CVs describe job duties. The strongest ones describe achievements.

**Weak:** "Responsible for serving customers and handling cash."

**Strong:** "Served an average of 80 customers per shift, maintaining accuracy on a $3,000 daily float. Recognised twice by management for customer satisfaction."

The formula: **action verb + what you did + result or scale**.

Use strong action verbs: managed, created, led, increased, reduced, designed, analysed, delivered, implemented.

Quantify wherever possible. Numbers are concrete and memorable. "Increased social media engagement by 40%" is more compelling than "improved social media performance."

## Tailoring your CV

Generic CVs perform worse than tailored ones. Before applying for each job:

- Read the job description carefully
- Note the specific skills, experience, and qualities they are asking for
- Make sure your CV reflects those things using similar language where honest
- Move the most relevant experience to the top of your work history section

This does not mean lying — it means emphasising what is most relevant.

## Common mistakes

**Too long** — more is not more. Cut ruthlessly.

**Generic summary** — "hardworking team player" says nothing. Either write something specific or leave it out.

**Spelling and grammar errors** — proofread multiple times. Ask someone else to read it. A typo in a CV for any role involving attention to detail is an immediate red flag.

**Wrong contact details** — double-check your phone number and email address. This happens more than you would think.

**Unprofessional email address** — create a professional email address if you do not have one. firstname.lastname@gmail.com is fine. cooldude99@hotmail.com is not.

**Listing every job from age 14** — you do not need to include every casual job if you have more relevant experience. Be selective.

**Using "I"** — bullet points in a CV do not start with "I". Not "I managed a team" but "Managed a team of five."

**Including a photo** — standard NZ practice is no photo on a CV.

## Cover letters

Many job listings still ask for a cover letter. If they do, write one — not submitting one when asked is an easy way to be eliminated.

A good cover letter is three paragraphs:
1. Why you want this specific job at this specific company (do your research)
2. Why you are the right person for it (two or three specific points from your experience)
3. A clear expression of enthusiasm and next step

It should not repeat your CV verbatim. It should complement it by adding context and personality.

## Once you have a strong CV

Keep it updated every few months even if you are not actively job hunting. It is much easier to add a role while the details are fresh than to reconstruct it six months later.

Save a master version with everything on it, then create tailored versions from it for each application. Never send the master version — it will be too long and not tailored.
