If you have a part-time job or are about to start one, some big changes to KiwiSaver now affect you directly. Whether you are stacking shelves, working in a cafe, or picking fruit on weekends, here is what has changed and why it matters.

## What actually changed?

From 1 April 2026, the default KiwiSaver contribution rate increased from 3% to 3.5% for both employees and employers. This is the first change to the default rate since KiwiSaver launched back in 2007. A further increase to 4% is planned for 1 April 2028.

But the bigger news for teens is this: for the first time, 16 and 17 year olds who are KiwiSaver members now receive compulsory employer contributions. Previously, employers were only required to contribute for workers aged 18 to 65. That has now changed.

On top of that, 16 and 17 year olds also became eligible for the annual government contribution from 1 July 2025. The government now contributes 25 cents for every dollar you put in, up to a maximum of $260.72 per year. To get the full amount, you need to contribute at least $1,042.86 in a year.

## What this means for your pay

When you are in KiwiSaver, 3.5% of your before-tax pay gets taken out each payday and put into your KiwiSaver account. Your employer then matches that with another 3.5% on top of your wages.

Here is a quick example. Say you earn $200 a week from your part-time job:

- **Your contribution:** $7 per week goes from your pay into KiwiSaver
- **Employer contribution:** Your employer puts in another $7 on top of your wages
- **Total going into your KiwiSaver:** $14 per week

Yes, your take-home pay drops by $7 a week. But your employer is adding another $7 that you would not get otherwise. That employer contribution is basically free money you only receive if you are in KiwiSaver.

## Why starting young is a big deal

The most powerful thing working in your favour right now is time. When your KiwiSaver money gets invested, the returns it earns start earning their own returns. This is called compounding, and over decades it makes a massive difference.

A young worker who starts contributing at 16 rather than waiting until 25 gives their savings an extra nine years to grow. Even small contributions from a part-time job in your teens can end up being some of the most valuable money you ever invest, simply because it has the longest time to compound.

## How to join KiwiSaver if you are under 18

If you are under 18, you cannot join KiwiSaver through your employer. You need to sign up directly through a KiwiSaver provider. If you are 16 or 17, you need at least one parent or legal guardian to co-sign your application.

Here is what to do:

1. **Choose a KiwiSaver provider.** There are over 20 providers in New Zealand. Look at fees, fund options, and track record. Websites like Sorted (sorted.org.nz) can help you compare.
2. **Complete the application form** with your parent or guardian.
3. **Tell your employer** you have joined KiwiSaver. They will then start deducting contributions from your pay and making their own employer contributions.

Once you are a member and working, your employer must contribute 3.5% automatically. You do not need to ask for it.

## Can you use KiwiSaver to buy your first home?

Yes. If you have been a KiwiSaver member for at least three years, you can withdraw most of your savings to put towards buying your first home. You need to leave $1,000 in your account, and the home must be one you intend to live in.

This is one of the biggest reasons to join early. If you sign up at 16, you could be eligible for a first home withdrawal by 19. That three-year clock starts ticking from your first contribution, so every year you wait is a year longer before you can access this benefit.

## What if 3.5% feels like too much?

If the 3.5% contribution feels too tight on your budget, you can apply to Inland Revenue for a temporary rate reduction to stay at 3%. This lasts between 3 and 12 months, and you can reapply as many times as you need. You apply through your myIR account.

However, think carefully before doing this. At 3.5%, your employer also contributes 3.5%. If you reduce your rate, your employer can choose to drop their contribution to match. That means less free money going into your account.

## What you should do right now

- **If you have a job and are not in KiwiSaver:** Talk to a parent or guardian about joining through a provider. The sooner you join, the sooner the three-year clock starts for a first home withdrawal.
- **If you are already in KiwiSaver:** Check your next payslip to make sure the 3.5% rate is being applied and your employer is contributing too.
- **If you are about to start your first job:** Sort out your KiwiSaver membership before or right when you start, so you do not miss out on employer contributions from day one.

The changes that kicked in on 1 April 2026 are genuinely good news for young workers. For the first time, teens are getting the same employer support that adult workers have always received. Take advantage of it — future you will be glad you did.
