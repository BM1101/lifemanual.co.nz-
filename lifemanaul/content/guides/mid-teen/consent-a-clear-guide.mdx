Consent is one of those topics that often gets taught in a way that feels clinical or uncomfortable. This guide treats it the way it deserves — as something genuinely important, explained plainly.

## What consent actually means

Consent is an agreement between people to participate in an activity together. In the context of physical or sexual activity, consent means:

**Freely given** — not because of pressure, manipulation, or fear.

**Reversible** — anyone can change their mind at any time, even if they said yes before.

**Enthusiastic** — genuine consent is not reluctant. If someone seems hesitant or like they are going along with something, that is not a clear yes.

**Specific** — saying yes to one thing does not mean yes to everything.

A useful way to think about it: consent is an enthusiastic yes, not the absence of a no.

## Asking for consent

Asking does not have to be awkward: "Is this okay?", "Do you want to?", "Are you comfortable with this?" Pay attention to both the words and the body language.

## Age of consent in New Zealand

The age of consent in New Zealand is 16. Sexual activity between two people where one or both are under 16 is illegal.

## If something has happened to you

If something has happened to you without your consent, it was not your fault. It does not matter what you were wearing, whether you had been drinking, or whether you said no clearly enough.
