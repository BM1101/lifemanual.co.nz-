If you have a part-time job and you're in KiwiSaver, your pay packet changed slightly from 1 April 2026. The default contribution rate went up, and if you're 16 or 17, some big new benefits kicked in too. Here's a plain-English breakdown of what happened and what you should do about it.

## What actually changed on 1 April 2026?

The default KiwiSaver contribution rate rose from 3% to 3.5% of your before-tax pay. This happened automatically — you didn't need to do anything.

That means two things:

- **You contribute more.** 3.5% of your gross pay now goes into your KiwiSaver account each payday, up from 3%.
- **Your employer contributes more too.** Your employer is now required to put in at least 3.5% on top of your wages.

This is the first step in a two-stage increase. The rate is scheduled to go up again to 4% from 1 April 2028.

## Big news if you're 16 or 17

Before April 2026, employers didn't have to contribute to KiwiSaver for workers under 18. That has changed. If you're 16 or 17 and you're a KiwiSaver member contributing from your wages, your employer must now contribute 3.5% of your pay as well.

On top of that, 16- and 17-year-olds became eligible for the annual government contribution from 1 July 2025. The government adds 25 cents for every dollar you put in, up to a maximum of $260.72 per year. To get the full amount, you need to contribute at least $1,042.86 during the KiwiSaver year (1 July to 30 June). Even if you don't hit that amount, you'll still get a partial top-up based on what you did put in.

That's essentially free money going into your account, on top of what your employer adds.

## How does this affect your take-home pay?

The extra 0.5% means you'll take home slightly less each payday. On a part-time wage, the difference will be small — often just a few dollars a week. But that money isn't gone. It's being invested for your future, and your employer is matching it.

Think of it this way: for every dollar of take-home pay you give up, your KiwiSaver balance grows by more than a dollar because of the employer match.

## What if you can't afford the higher rate?

If the increase feels like a stretch right now, you have an option. You can apply to Inland Revenue for a temporary rate reduction to stay at 3%.

Here's how it works:

- You apply through your myIR account at ird.govt.nz
- You don't need to prove financial hardship — anyone can apply
- The reduction lasts between 3 and 12 months
- After it expires, your rate goes back to 3.5% automatically
- You can reapply as many times as you like

One thing to be aware of: if you reduce your rate to 3%, your employer can also choose to drop their contribution to 3%. That means you'd miss out on the extra 0.5% from them as well. Whether they match your reduced rate or stick with 3.5% is up to them.

## Why starting early matters so much

When you're a teenager, retirement feels impossibly far away. But that distance is actually your biggest advantage. Your money has decades to grow through compounding — where your investment returns earn their own returns, year after year.

A young worker who starts contributing at 16 instead of 25 gives their savings an extra nine years of growth. Even small amounts from a part-time job can become some of the most valuable dollars you ever invest, simply because they have so much time to compound.

KiwiSaver isn't just for retirement either. After three years as a member, you can withdraw your savings to help buy your first home. If you join at 16, you could be eligible for a first-home withdrawal by 19.

## What you should do right now

- **Check your payslip.** Make sure 3.5% is being deducted. If something looks wrong, talk to your employer or payroll team.
- **Stay in KiwiSaver.** Opting out means walking away from your employer's contributions and the government top-up. That's real money you lose.
- **Think about your fund type.** If you won't need your KiwiSaver money for many years, a growth fund may suit you better than a conservative one. Your KiwiSaver provider can help you understand the options.
- **Consider topping up before 30 June.** If you haven't contributed $1,042.86 this KiwiSaver year, even a small voluntary top-up before 30 June means you'll receive a bigger government contribution.

## Where to get help

- **Inland Revenue:** ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver-changes or call 0800 549 472
- **Sorted:** sorted.org.nz — a free, independent money guide from the NZ government
- **Your KiwiSaver provider:** log in to your account to check your balance, fund type, and contribution rate

The shift to 3.5% might feel small, but over a working lifetime it adds up significantly. The best thing you can do right now is stay in, stay informed, and let time do the heavy lifting.
