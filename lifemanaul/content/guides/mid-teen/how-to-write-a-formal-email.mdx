Being able to write a clear, well-structured email is a skill that will serve you throughout your career. Most people do not write emails particularly well — which means doing it well gets noticed.

## The basic structure

**Subject line** — specific and descriptive. "Question about the job" is vague. "Application for Saturday shift — Bella Morrison" is clear.

**Greeting** — "Dear [Name]," for formal emails. "Hi [Name]," for semi-formal contexts. Never "Hey" or no greeting at all.

**Opening line** — brief context: "I am writing to enquire about..."

**Body** — your actual message. One paragraph per idea.

**Request or next step** — "Could you please let me know if..." or "I would appreciate it if you could..."

**Closing** — "Kind regards," or "Thank you," followed by your full name.

## An example

**Bad:**
> Subject: hi
> Hey Mr Thompson, I was wondering about my essay mark. Let me know. Thanks, Jake

**Good:**
> Subject: Essay feedback — Jake Williams, English 101
> Dear Mr Thompson, I hope you are well. I am writing to ask whether I could get some feedback on my recent essay. Would it be possible to arrange a brief meeting, or to receive written feedback if that is easier? Thank you for your time. Kind regards, Jake Williams

## Key rules

- Use full sentences — no text speak
- Keep it short — most formal emails should be under 150 words
- One clear request — make it explicit
- Proofread before sending — check names are spelled correctly
- Reply within 24 to 48 hours when someone emails you

## Subject lines

Good: "Work experience enquiry — Emma Tait, Rangitoto College"
Poor: "Hi", "Question", "Following up"

## Following up

If you have not received a reply after a week, one follow-up is appropriate: "I wanted to follow up on my email from [date] regarding [topic]. Please let me know if you need any additional information."
