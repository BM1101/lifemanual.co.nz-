A bank account is one of the first genuinely adult things you need. Without one you cannot get paid, pay for things online, or participate in the modern financial system.

## What age can you open an account?

Most NZ banks let you open a basic account from age 13 with a parent or guardian present. From age 16, you can generally open an account in your own name without parental involvement. Ask specifically about youth or student accounts — these typically have no monthly fees.

## What you will need

**Photo ID** — a passport is best. If you do not have one, a birth certificate plus another form of ID usually works.

**Proof of address** — a letter addressed to you at your home address. A bank statement, utility bill, or letter from school works. If you live with your parents, a letter from them confirming your address is generally accepted.

**IRD number** — your tax number. You can still open the account without one and apply separately.

**A parent or guardian** — required if you are under 16.

## Which bank?

The five main retail banks in NZ are ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Kiwibank, and Westpac. More important than which bank: does it have a branch near you, how is the app, and are there fees? For under-18s, most banks offer completely fee-free accounts. Confirm this before signing up.

## Transaction account vs savings account

You want two accounts:

**Transaction account** — where your money lives day-to-day. Your pay goes in, your spending comes out. Comes with an EFTPOS or debit card.

**Savings account** — a separate account for money you are not planning to spend. You usually do not get a card for it, which helps you leave it alone.

Most banks let you open both at the same time. Do it.

## Things to watch out for

**Monthly fees** — for under-18s, you should pay nothing. Always ask about youth options.

**Overdraft** — some accounts automatically approve small overdrafts. Ask to have it disabled if you want to avoid the temptation.

## After you open your account

Set up internet banking, note your account number for future employers, activate your card, and consider setting up an automatic transfer to savings — even $10 a week builds a habit over time.
