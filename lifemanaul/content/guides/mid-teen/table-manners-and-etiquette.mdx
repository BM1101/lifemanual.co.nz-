Table manners communicate whether you are considerate of the people around you. That impression follows you into every social and professional context.

## The basics that cover almost everything

**Wait for everyone to be served** before eating, or for the host to indicate it is okay to start.

**Chew with your mouth closed.**

**No phones at the table** — or if your phone is out, it is face down and you are not checking it.

**Do not talk with your mouth full.**

**Do not reach across people** — ask for things to be passed.

**Say please and thank you** — to the host, to restaurant staff.

## At a restaurant

Know what you want before the server arrives. Do not order the most or least expensive thing on the menu if someone else is paying. If splitting the bill, the clearest approach is to split equally unless someone has ordered significantly more.

Tipping in NZ is appreciated but not expected — unlike the US. If the service was good, 10% is generous.

## Formal dining

**Cutlery:** Work from the outside in. The cutlery furthest from your plate is for the first course.

**Bread:** Tear with your hands, do not cut. Butter only a small piece at a time.

**Glasses:** Your bread plate is on the left, your drinks are on the right. Memory trick: BMW — bread, meal, water.

**Napkin:** Goes in your lap when you sit down. If you leave briefly, leave it on your chair. At the end of the meal, leave it loosely folded to the left of your plate.

## When you are the guest

Compliment the food even if it is not your favourite. Offer to help clear up. Thank them genuinely before you leave.

## Why this matters

Etiquette is not about performing for appearances. It is about signalling awareness of the people around you. The specific rules vary across cultures — what matters is the underlying mindset of thinking about others, not just yourself.
