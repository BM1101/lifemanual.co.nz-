Getting your first pay is exciting. Spending it all immediately is extremely easy. Building the habit of managing money well from the start is one of the most valuable things you can do.

## The 50/30/20 rule

- **50% on needs** — things you genuinely need (food, transport, essential clothing)
- **30% on wants** — things you enjoy but do not strictly need (eating out, entertainment)
- **20% on saving** — money you set aside and do not spend

Saving is not optional — it comes off the top, not after everything else.

## Pay yourself first

When you get paid, immediately move your savings amount into your savings account before you do anything else. Most people try to save what is left over. There is almost never anything left over. If you move the money first, you adjust your spending to what remains.

Even $20 a pay cycle, moved automatically when your pay arrives, builds a habit and a balance over time.

## A simple system

**Two accounts** — a transaction account for day-to-day spending, and a savings account you treat as untouchable.

**One weekly check-in** — spend five minutes looking at what you spent. Most banking apps show spending broken down by category.

**One question before buying** — "do I actually want this, or am I just bored or impulse buying?" This single pause prevents a significant amount of regret.

## A note on buy now, pay later

Services like Afterpay let you buy something and pay in instalments. They are fine if you use them for things you were already going to buy and can genuinely afford — but multiple BNPL commitments stack up invisibly. If you use them, keep it to one at a time.

## Goals make saving easier

Saving for a specific thing — a trip, a laptop, a car — is much easier than saving "for the future." Even $10 a week toward a $500 goal is visible momentum.
