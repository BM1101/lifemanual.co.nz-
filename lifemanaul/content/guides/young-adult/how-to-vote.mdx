## Why younger people not voting matters

In NZ elections, voters aged 18 to 34 participate at significantly lower rates than older voters. Politicians know this — they have an incentive to design policies that appeal to older, reliable voters. When young people do not vote, they hand their share of influence to other people.

## Enrolling to vote

Go to vote.nz. You need to be a NZ citizen or permanent resident, aged 18 or over. Update your enrolment whenever you move address.

## How MMP works

You get two votes:

**Electorate vote** — for a specific candidate in your local electorate.

**Party vote** — for a political party. This determines the overall composition of Parliament. A party that receives 5% of the party vote gets seats proportional to that share.

Your party vote is usually more important for most parties — it directly determines how many MPs they have in total.

## Reading the ballot paper

You receive two ballot papers — green (electorate) and orange (party). Vote for one person on the green paper and one party on the orange paper. You can split your vote.

## On election day

Polling places are open 9am to 7pm. Find yours at vote.nz. You can also vote in advance at various locations for several weeks beforehand.

## Local elections

NZ also has local government elections every three years. These decide things that affect you directly — local planning, public transport, rates. Turnout is often under 40% — your vote carries more weight here than anywhere.
