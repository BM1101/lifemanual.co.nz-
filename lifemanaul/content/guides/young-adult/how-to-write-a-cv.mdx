Your CV is not a biography. It is a marketing document with one job: to get you an interview.

## How long

Entry-level roles: one page. More experienced: two pages maximum.

## The format that works

Use a clean, simple layout. No photos, no graphics, no coloured text. Black text on white, readable font at 10 to 12 point.

**Contact details** — name, phone, email, location (city only).

**Work experience** — reverse chronological order. For each role: job title, company, dates, and three to five bullet points describing what you did and achieved.

**Education** — school, qualification, year completed.

**Skills** — relevant technical skills only. Do not list soft skills like "team player."

## Writing your work experience

The weakest CVs describe job duties. The strongest describe achievements.

**Weak:** "Responsible for serving customers and handling cash."

**Strong:** "Served an average of 80 customers per shift, maintaining accuracy on a $3,000 daily float."

Formula: action verb plus what you did plus result or scale.

## Common mistakes

- Too long
- Spelling and grammar errors — proofread multiple times
- Unprofessional email address — firstname.lastname@gmail.com is fine
- Using "I" — bullet points do not start with "I"
- Including a photo — standard NZ practice is no photo

## Tailoring your CV

Read the job description carefully. Make sure your CV reflects what they are asking for using similar language where honest. Generic CVs perform worse than tailored ones.
