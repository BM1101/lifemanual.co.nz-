About one in five New Zealanders experiences a mental health problem in any given year. That number is higher for young adults. Anxiety, depression, and stress-related conditions are extremely common — not a sign of weakness, and not something you just have to endure.

## How to know if you need support

If you are wondering whether what you are experiencing is serious enough to get help, that question itself is a reason to talk to someone. Some signs it is time: persistent low mood for more than two weeks, anxiety significantly affecting your daily life, difficulty sleeping, using alcohol or substances more than you would like, or thoughts of self-harm.

## Starting with your GP

Your GP is the best first point of contact. They can assess your symptoms, prescribe medication if appropriate, and refer you to funded services. Be honest — describe what you are actually experiencing, not a minimised version.

## Free and low-cost options

**1737** — free, 24/7 counselling by phone or text. Text or call 1737 any time.

**Primary Mental Health services** — many GP practices have access to funded counselling sessions for mild to moderate conditions. Ask your GP.

**Student health services** — if you are a tertiary student, your institution likely has low or no-cost counselling. Under-utilised and worth using.

**EAP** — if you are employed, your employer may have an Employee Assistance Programme offering free confidential counselling sessions. Ask HR.

**Youthline** — 0800 376 633 or text 234.

## Private therapy

Sessions typically cost $100 to $200 per hour. Ask therapists about sliding scale fees based on income — many offer them. Community counselling organisations often provide subsidised rates.

## If you or someone you know is in crisis

Call 111 if there is immediate risk of harm. Otherwise: 1737, Lifeline (0800 543 354), or Suicide Crisis Helpline (0508 828 865).
