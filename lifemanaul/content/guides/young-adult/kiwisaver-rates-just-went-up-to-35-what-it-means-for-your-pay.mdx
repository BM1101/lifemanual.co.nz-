If your pay packet has felt a bit lighter since April, you are not imagining things. The default KiwiSaver contribution rate increased from 3% to 3.5% on 1 April 2026 — for both you and your employer. The change happened automatically, so if you were on the default rate, more of your pay is now going straight into your KiwiSaver fund.

Here is what actually changed, how much it costs you in take-home pay, and how to decide whether to apply for the temporary reduction back to 3%.

## What changed on 1 April 2026

The minimum KiwiSaver contribution rate for employees and employers went up from 3% to 3.5% of your before-tax pay. This is the first step in a two-stage increase — the rate will rise again to 4% on 1 April 2028.

If you were already contributing at 4%, 6%, 8%, or 10%, nothing changed for your employee contribution. However, if your employer was only contributing the old minimum of 3%, their contribution also rose to 3.5%.

You did not need to do anything for the new rate to apply. It updated automatically through your employer's payroll.

## How much does 0.5% actually cost?

On a $55,000 salary, the jump from 3% to 3.5% means an extra $275 per year going to KiwiSaver — roughly $5.29 per week less in your bank account. Your employer also puts in an extra $275 per year on their side, so your KiwiSaver balance grows by $550 more each year before investment returns.

On a $70,000 salary, you would contribute $2,450 per year at 3.5% — about $47 per week — compared to $2,100 at 3% ($40 per week). That is around $7 per week less take-home pay.

It might not sound like much, but for young workers already stretched by rent, groceries, and a student loan repayment, every dollar counts.

## The government contribution has also been cut

Since 1 July 2025, the government contribution has been halved. It is now 25 cents for every dollar you contribute, up to a maximum of $260.72 per year. It used to be 50 cents per dollar, up to $521.43.

To receive the full $260.72, you need to personally contribute at least $1,042.86 between 1 July and 30 June each year. If you are earning over roughly $30,000 and contributing at 3.5%, you will hit this threshold through your regular pay without needing to do anything extra.

People earning over $180,000 per year are no longer eligible for the government contribution at all.

## You can apply for a temporary rate reduction

If the increase is squeezing your budget, you can apply to Inland Revenue (IR) for a temporary rate reduction back to 3%. Here are the key details:

- You apply through your myIR account — it is straightforward and online
- The reduction lasts for a minimum of 3 months and a maximum of 12 months
- You can reapply as many times as you like — there is no limit
- This is not a hardship application — you do not need to prove financial difficulty
- When the period ends, your rate automatically resets to the default 3.5% unless you reapply or choose a different rate
- Once approved, IR sends you an acceptance letter with start and end dates to show your employer

## The catch: your employer might reduce their contribution too

This is the part many people miss. If you opt for the temporary reduction to 3%, your employer can choose to also drop their contribution from 3.5% to 3%. That means you could lose 1% of your gross salary flowing into KiwiSaver (0.5% from your side plus 0.5% from your employer's side). Not every employer will do this, but they are allowed to.

Before applying, it is worth asking your employer whether they would keep contributing 3.5% even if you temporarily drop to 3%.

## Should you take the reduction? A simple decision framework

**Stay at 3.5% if:**
- You can absorb $5 to $10 less per week without real hardship
- You want to maximise your employer's matching contribution
- You are saving for your first home through KiwiSaver and want a larger balance
- You are in your 20s or 30s and have decades for compound returns to work

**Apply for the reduction to 3% if:**
- You are genuinely struggling to cover essentials like rent, food, or power
- You have high-interest debt (such as credit cards or buy-now-pay-later) that needs paying down first
- You are on a very low or variable income and need flexibility right now
- You plan to increase your rate again once things stabilise

There is no shame in temporarily reducing. The option exists specifically so people are not forced into financial stress. The key word is "temporarily" — come back to 3.5% (or higher) as soon as you can.

## What to do right now

1. **Check your payslip.** Make sure your KiwiSaver deduction is showing the correct rate — either 3.5% or 3% if you have already applied for the reduction. If something looks wrong, talk to your payroll team.
2. **Log in to myIR.** If you want to apply for the temporary reduction, you can do this at ird.govt.nz through your myIR account at any time.
3. **Ask your employer.** Find out whether they would continue contributing 3.5% if you reduce to 3%. This could change your decision.
4. **Think long-term.** Even a small amount now can make a big difference over 30 or 40 years of compounding. If you can manage the slightly smaller pay packet, keeping the higher rate is almost always worth it.

The rate is going up again to 4% in April 2028, so getting used to 3.5% now makes the next step easier. Future you will be grateful.
