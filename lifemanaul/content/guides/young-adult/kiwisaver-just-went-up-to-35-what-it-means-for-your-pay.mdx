If your latest payslip looked a little lighter than usual, you're not imagining things. From 1 April 2026, the default KiwiSaver contribution rate increased from 3% to 3.5% — both for you and your employer. Here's what that means in plain language, and what you can do about it if money is tight right now.

## What actually changed

The government lifted the default KiwiSaver contribution rate as part of Budget 2025. If you were on the old 3% default rate, your contributions automatically moved up to 3.5% from your first payday in April. Your employer's minimum contribution also went up to 3.5%.

You didn't need to fill in any forms or call anyone — it just happened. A further increase to 4% is already scheduled for 1 April 2028.

If you had already chosen a higher rate (like 4%, 6%, 8% or 10%), your employee contribution stayed the same. However, if your employer was only contributing the old minimum of 3%, their contribution has now risen to 3.5%.

## How it affects your take-home pay

The extra 0.5% comes out of your gross (before-tax) pay. Here's roughly what that looks like across common salary levels:

- **Earning $40,000/year:** about $200 more per year going into KiwiSaver, or roughly $8 less per fortnight in your bank account
- **Earning $55,000/year:** about $275 more per year, or roughly $11 less per fortnight
- **Earning $70,000/year:** about $350 more per year, or roughly $13 less per fortnight

The flip side? Your employer is also putting in an extra 0.5%, so your KiwiSaver balance grows faster from both sides. Over decades, that compounding adds up to a meaningful difference at retirement.

## If you're 16 or 17 — this matters to you too

From 1 April 2026, employers are now required to make KiwiSaver contributions for 16- and 17-year-old employees who are existing KiwiSaver members. Previously, employer contributions only kicked in at age 18.

On top of that, 16- and 17-year-olds now also qualify for the government contribution of up to $260.72 per year. To get the full amount, you need to contribute at least $1,042.86 between 1 July and 30 June. If you're working part-time, you might not hit that through wages alone, but you can always top up with a voluntary payment to your provider before 30 June.

## How to apply for a temporary rate reduction

If the higher rate is squeezing your budget, you can apply to stay at 3% temporarily. This is not a hardship application — you don't need to prove financial difficulty.

Here's how it works:

1. **Log in to myIR** at ird.govt.nz (or call Inland Revenue, or send them a web message in myIR)
2. **Apply for a temporary rate reduction** — you choose a period between 3 and 12 months
3. **Receive your acceptance letter** from Inland Revenue showing the start and end dates
4. **Give the letter to your employer** so they can update your payroll

Once the temporary period ends, your rate automatically goes back to the default 3.5%. You can reapply as many times as you like, and you can cancel the reduction early if your situation improves.

One thing to be aware of: if you drop back to 3%, your employer can also choose to match at 3% instead of 3.5%. That means you could end up with 1% less going into your KiwiSaver account overall (your 0.5% plus their 0.5%). It's worth asking your employer what they'll do before you apply.

## Should you stick with 3.5% or reduce?

There's no single right answer — it depends on your situation right now. Here are some things to think about:

- **Stick with 3.5% if you can.** The drop in take-home pay is relatively small, and the extra savings compound over your whole working life. If you're in your late teens or twenties, even small increases now can make a big difference at 65.
- **Consider reducing temporarily** if you're genuinely struggling with rent, debt, or essential costs. Getting on top of high-interest debt first can sometimes make more financial sense.
- **Don't opt out of KiwiSaver altogether** just because of a tight month. A temporary rate reduction keeps you saving and keeps you eligible for the government contribution.

If you earn more than about $30,000 a year and contribute at least 3.5%, you'll automatically hit the $1,042.86 threshold needed to get the full government contribution of $260.72 each year. Dropping to 3% on a lower income could mean missing out on some of that free money.

## What's coming next

The default rate will rise again to 4% on 1 April 2028 for both employees and employers. Getting comfortable with 3.5% now gives you time to adjust before the next step up.

You can check your current contribution rate and KiwiSaver balance any time through myIR, or by contacting your KiwiSaver provider. If you're not sure whether your settings are right for you, the government's free Sorted.org.nz KiwiSaver calculator is a good place to start — or talk to a financial mentor through MoneyTalks (0800 345 123).
