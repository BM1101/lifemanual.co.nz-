## Simple vs compound interest

Simple interest earns on the original amount only. Compound interest earns on the original amount and all the interest already earned. The difference seems small early on — over decades it becomes extraordinary.

$10,000 at 7% simple interest after 20 years: $24,000. At 7% compound interest: $38,697.

## The Rule of 72

Divide 72 by your annual return rate to find how many years it takes to double your money.

At 7%: doubles every 10.3 years. So $10,000 invested at 18 becomes $20,000 by 28, $40,000 by 38, $80,000 by 48, $160,000 by 58 — without adding another dollar.

## Why your twenties matter more than your forties

Two people invest the same amount monthly at the same return rate. Alex starts at 22, Jordan starts at 32 and invests twice as much to catch up. By age 65, Alex ends up with more — despite investing less in dollar terms. The early years cannot be bought back.

## Where compound interest works against you

A credit card at 20% annual interest compounds just as aggressively. $5,000 left unpaid for 10 years becomes $30,959. This is why paying off high-interest debt is almost always the right move before investing.

## What this means practically

Start now. Whatever you can. Set up an automatic investment so the decision does not rely on monthly willpower. Then leave it alone and let time work.

The best time to start was ten years ago. The second best time is today.
