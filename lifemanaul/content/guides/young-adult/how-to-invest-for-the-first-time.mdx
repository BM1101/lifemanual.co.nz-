## Step one: sort KiwiSaver first

Your employer contributes at least 3% of your gross wages. The government also contributes up to $521 per year if you contribute at least $1,042 yourself. That combination is the best return on investment available to most New Zealanders. Maximise it first.

Also check which KiwiSaver fund you are in. Most young people should be in a growth or aggressive fund — not a conservative one.

## What is an index fund?

An index fund automatically tracks a market index. When you invest in one, you own a tiny fraction of every company in that index — instant diversification and very low fees (typically 0.1 to 0.5% annually).

## Where to invest in NZ

**Sharesies** — start with $1. Clean app, beginner-friendly. $3 per month for portfolios under $50,000.

**InvestNow** — no platform fees. Minimum $250 per fund. Invest directly with fund managers.

**Kernel** — NZ-built, low-cost index funds. Around 0.25% annual fee, no transaction fees.

All three are regulated by the Financial Markets Authority.

## What to actually buy

For most first-time investors: a global index fund. On Sharesies, look for Smartshares Total World. On InvestNow or Kernel, look for a global equities index fund with fees below 0.5%.

One diversified global fund is enough to start. Add complexity later.

## How much to invest

Set up an automatic contribution — even $25 per week. Pay yourself first: move the investment amount when your pay arrives, then spend what is left.

## What to know about risk

Your balance will go down sometimes. The correct response to a market downturn when you have a long time horizon is to do nothing — or keep contributing at the lower prices. Selling when markets fall locks in your loss.
