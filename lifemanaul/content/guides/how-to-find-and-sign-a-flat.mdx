Flatting for the first time is exciting and occasionally overwhelming. The single most important thing you can do before moving in is understand what you are signing — and what rights you have once you do.

## Finding a flat

The main platforms for finding rentals in NZ are Trade Me Property (trademe.co.nz/property) and Homes.co.nz. Facebook Marketplace and local Facebook groups also have listings, particularly for flatmates rather than whole properties.

**What to look for when viewing:**

- Test hot water, taps, and any appliances included
- Check windows open and close and have functioning latches
- Look for signs of dampness or mould — check corners, under sinks, around windows
- Check heating — does the property have it? What type? Running costs matter
- Check phone and internet coverage
- Ask about parking, storage, and rubbish collection
- Meet your potential flatmates if applicable — you will be living with these people

**Ask the landlord:**

- What is included in rent (water, lawn mowing, etc.)?
- Who is responsible for what maintenance?
- What is the process for reporting repairs?
- Why is the current tenant leaving?

## The tenancy agreement

A tenancy agreement is a legally binding contract. Once you sign it, you are bound by it. Read every clause before signing. Key things to check:

**Type of tenancy** — most NZ residential tenancies are periodic (ongoing, no fixed end date) or fixed-term (a specific period, after which it reverts to periodic or ends). Fixed-term tenancies are harder to exit early.

**Rent amount and when it is due** — when does rent start? What date is it due? What is the payment method?

**Bond** — the bond is a security deposit, usually 2 to 4 weeks rent. By law, it must be lodged with Tenancy Services (not kept by the landlord). You should receive a receipt.

**What is included** — appliances, furnishings, parking, outdoor areas.

**Rules** — some landlords prohibit pets or smoking. These must be specified in the agreement to be enforceable.

If anything in the agreement seems unusual or you do not understand it, ask before signing. Citizens Advice Bureau (cab.org.nz) offers free, independent advice on tenancy agreements.

## Your rights as a tenant

The Residential Tenancies Act 1986 (and its amendments) governs almost everything about your tenancy. Key rights:

**Right to quiet enjoyment** — you have the right to live in your home without interference. Your landlord cannot enter without proper notice.

**Notice before entry** — your landlord must give at least 24 hours notice before entering your home, except in genuine emergencies (fire, flood, etc.). If they show up unannounced, you can ask them to leave.

**Repairs and maintenance** — your landlord is responsible for keeping the property in reasonable repair. If something breaks or becomes unsafe, notify them in writing (text or email creates a record). They must respond promptly.

**Healthy homes standards** — NZ rental properties must meet minimum standards for heating, insulation, ventilation, moisture control, and draught stopping. Your landlord must provide a current Healthy Homes compliance statement.

**Rent increases** — rent can only be increased once every 12 months and the landlord must give 60 days notice.

## Your responsibilities as a tenant

Tenancy is a two-way relationship. As a tenant you are responsible for:

- Paying rent on time
- Keeping the property reasonably clean and tidy
- Not damaging the property (beyond fair wear and tear)
- Notifying the landlord promptly about any maintenance issues
- Following reasonable rules specified in the agreement

## The bond

When you move out, the bond should be returned in full unless there is damage beyond fair wear and tear, or rent is owed. If your landlord wants to make deductions, they must apply to Tenancy Services.

If you disagree with deductions, you can challenge them through the Tenancy Tribunal — a free dispute resolution service.

**Document everything on move-in** — take timestamped photos of every room, every wall, every appliance before you unpack anything. This evidence is invaluable if there is a dispute about the bond when you leave.

## Ending a tenancy

**Periodic tenancy:** You must give 28 days notice to end a periodic tenancy. Your landlord must give 63 days notice (or 90 days if they are selling the property or moving in themselves).

**Fixed-term tenancy:** You generally cannot end a fixed-term tenancy early without the landlord's agreement — and they may charge you for any costs of re-letting the property.

## Getting help

**Tenancy Services:** tenancy.govt.nz or 0800 836 262 — free advice on tenancy law
**Tenancy Tribunal:** resolves disputes between landlords and tenants for free
**Citizens Advice Bureau:** free general legal advice, including tenancy issues
